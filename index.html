How to Play

• Objective: Move all 15 checkers to your Home Board and bear them off before your opponent.

• Interaction: Click or tap a checker (or its point) to select it, then click the point where it should go. On touch screens the legal destinations are tinted. Keyboard: Tab to a movable point, Enter to select, Tab to a destination, Enter to move. Shortcuts: R roll, U undo, H hint.

• Who starts: Only at the start of a match: each player rolls both dice (online, each player clicks Roll Dice for their own). A double wins; if both roll doubles the higher double wins; otherwise the higher total wins. A tie is rolled again. The winner starts by playing the dice they just rolled (a double is played four times), so nobody rolls again. Every later game of the match starts with the winner of the previous game, who simply rolls and plays.

• Hint: The lightbulb button works out a good way to play your roll and pulses the first checker to move and where it goes. Hints are only available against the computer, not in online games.

• Win chance: The switch above the board shows each side's chance to win this game. It plays the position out 1,000 times with a quick computer player and updates after every roll and move. It is an estimate (usually within a few percent); hovering over it with a mouse also shows the chances of a gammon.

• Saved: Your settings, and an unfinished match against the computer, are remembered if you close the page.

• Both dice at once: Select a checker and click a point that needs both dice (or several dice on doubles). The checker travels there in one move, as long as every step along the way is legal. Undo takes the whole move back.

• Animation: Checkers glide smoothly from point to point.

• Rules: Full rules apply including doubles (4 moves), hitting blots, and mandatory bar re-entry.

• Online: Press Play Online, create a game and send the link (or the 6-character code) to a friend. Once you are connected you both choose the match length and turn timer, and the game starts when you confirm the same settings; colours are drawn at random. Dice are thrown by the host's browser. If the connection drops, the game pauses and reconnects by itself (you can even reload the page and resume). An optional turn timer skips the turn of a player who runs out of time, and when a match ends either player can ask for a rematch.

• Watching: Once both players have started an online game, anyone with the game code, or the spectator link from Settings, can press Watch and follow it live: the board, the dice and the score. Spectators can join at any time but can't play, and the players see how many people are watching. In Settings a spectator can choose whose side of the board is drawn at the bottom.

• After a match with spectators: Each player chooses Keep playing, Watch or Quit. If both keep playing, it's a rematch. If a player stops, the spectators can take the free seat; the new pair then chooses the settings for their match, and everyone else watches it. The player who created the game should stay on the page until the next game is set up, because the game room runs on that page.

• Giving up: Once no contact is left and your opponent will finish first whatever the dice show, you're offered to give the game up, but only when playing on can't get you a better result. It costs the best result you can still reach: a single game (1 point) once you've borne off a checker; a gammon (2) once you can no longer bear a checker off in time, even with a checker still in your opponent's home board as long as it could still get out; a backgammon (3) only when it can't. While you could still save the gammon there's no offer. The flag button lets you give up at any time while the offer stands.

• Scoring: A win scores 1 point. A gammon (the loser has borne off no checkers) scores 2. A backgammon (a gammon where the loser still has a checker on the bar or in your home board) scores 3. The first player to reach the match target wins the match.
